While the foregoing words (vide verses
21—23) were being sympathetically uttered
by the gods, Gandharvas and Caranas, the
army of the ogres, armed with fearful shields
and weapons and distinguished by equally

fearful ensigns, closed in on all sides, making
a deep roar. The highly tumultuous noise of
those ogres—who were uttering heroic
slogans, approaching one another to give
instructions in tactics of war, twanging their
bows and bounding again and again in their
drunkenness, making clamorous sounds and
also beating their drums—filled that forest.
Terribly frightened by that noise, the wild beasts
of prey ran away to a far-off place where the
sound could not be heard, and did not look
behind. Nay, wielding various weapons, that
army of great impetuosity, deep like the ocean,
headed towards Rama. Casting his eyes on
all sides, and moving forward to give battle,
Sri Rama too, who was skilled in warfare,
surveyed that army of Khara. Nay, drawing
at full length his terrible bow and pulling out
arrows from his quiver, he summoned (to his
aid) violent anger for the destruction of the
ogres; and burning with anger, he was dreadful
to look at like fire at the time of universal
dissolution. (27—34)
