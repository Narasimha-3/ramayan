शुक्लानंशुमतश्चापि दन्तधावनसंचयान्‌।
शुक्लांश्वन्दनकल्कांश्व. समुद्रेष्ववतिष्ठत: ॥ ७५॥
दर्पणान्‌ परिमृष्ठांश्र वाससां चापि संचयान्‌।
पादुकोपानह चैव युग्मान्यत्र सहस्त्रश:॥ ७६॥
आज्जनी: कड्ड्तान्‌ कूर्चाश्छत्राणि च धनूंषि च।
मर्मत्राणानि चित्राणि शयनान्यासनानि च॥ ७७॥
प्रतिपानहृदान्‌ पूर्णान्‌ खरोष्टूगजवाजिनाम्‌।
अवगाहासुतीर्थाश्व॒ हृदान्‌ सोत्पलपुष्करान्‌।
आकाशवर्णप्रतिमान्‌ स्वच्छतोयान्‌ सुखाप्लवानू॥ ७८ ॥
नीलवैदूर्यवर्णाश्च॒ ye यवससंचयान्‌।
निर्वापार्थ पशूनां ते ददृशुस्तत्र सर्वशः॥ ७९॥
They further beheld at every place in
that area bundles of fresh white twigs with
their one end crushed for being used as a
tooth-brush, as well as the paste of white
sandalwood for being used as a shampoo,
kept in round covered wooden cases,
perfectly cleaned mirrors as well as piles of
textiles, as also thousands of pairs of wooden
sandals and leather shoes, small caskets
containing collyrium, combs, brushes for
combing moustaches and beards, nay,
umbrellas, and bows, armour and different
kinds of beds and seats, tanks full of water
worth drinking, feed for donkeys, camels,
elephants and horses, as well as tanks,
provided with excellent descents, where one
could easily take a plunge, full of lilies and
lotuses, resembling the sky in hue, containing
limpid water and pleasing to bathe in and
heaps of tender grass resembling the blue
cat’s-eyes gem in hue for the consumption
of animals. (75—79)
